The short version#

This policy covers our website, the messages you send us, and the practices that apply across our company. Each of our apps has its own privacy policy, linked below, that says exactly what that app does with your information. Where an app's policy and this one differ, the app's policy controls for that app. Our website has no accounts, no ads, and no analytics or tracking tools. We do not sell your personal information or share it for targeted advertising.

1. Who we are#

Thomas Ray Publishing LLC ("TRP", "we", "us", or "our") is an independent software developer and publisher based in the United States and registered in Nevada. We publish iOS, iPadOS, macOS, and watchOS apps, illustrated books, art collections, and iMessage sticker packs. For any privacy question or request, write to contact@thomasraypublishing.com or to the mailing address in Section 17. For people in Quebec, the person in charge of the protection of personal information under Quebec Law 25 is Sean Dryden, reachable at the same address.

2. What this policy covers#

This policy covers (a) our website, thomasraypublishing.com; (b) email and other messages you send us; and (c) the company-wide practices described in Section 4. Each app's privacy policy describes the information that app collects, why, and who receives it. The current app policies are:

  • Pomagotchi Privacy Policy
  • The Device — Cosmic Pyramid privacy addendum (on this page)
  • Colophon privacy addendum (on this page)
  • Hush Hush Snap Snap Privacy Policy
  • Trade RC Privacy Policy and the Trade RC Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy, which explains how that app handles health information under Washington, Nevada, and similar laws

3. Information we receive#

From our website. The website has no accounts, ads, analytics, or tracking tools, and it sets no cookies. Section 13 describes the limited technical information involved in delivering it.

When you write to us. If you email us, we receive your email address, your name if you give it, and whatever you write or attach. We use it to answer you and to keep a record of the request.

From our apps. What an app collects depends on the app. Some apps keep everything on your device. Some send technical and usage reports. One, Trade RC, has accounts and uses outside service providers. Each app's privacy policy lists what that app collects and who receives it, and that policy controls for that app.

From Apple. When you buy something in one of our apps, Apple processes the payment. We receive transaction and subscription status information from Apple. We do not receive your payment card details.

4. Practices that apply across our apps#

  • No advertising or cross-app tracking. None of our apps contains advertising tools, uses the advertising identifier, or tracks you across other companies' apps or websites.
  • No sale of personal information. We do not sell personal information, and we do not share it for targeted advertising.
  • On-device first. Where a feature can run on your device, we build it that way. Information an app stores only on your device, or in your own private iCloud storage, is not visible to us.
  • Service providers. Some of our apps use outside service providers, such as Apple, a hosting provider, a chat provider, or an AI provider. Each app's privacy policy names the providers that app uses and what they receive.
  • Usage and diagnostic reports. Some apps send technical and usage reports to a database we operate with Apple's CloudKit service. These reports are pseudonymous: they carry a random identifier for the app installation, and Apple attaches an identifier for the iCloud account that created the record. We do not use them to identify or contact you. Each app's policy says whether it sends these reports and how to turn them off.
  • Artificial intelligence. Each app's policy says whether the app uses an AI model, whether that model runs on your device or with an outside provider, and what is sent.

5. Where information comes from#

The personal information we hold comes from you (when you email us or send feedback), from Apple (purchase and subscription status), and from our apps (usage and diagnostic reports where they are turned on, and the account information described in an app's own policy).

6. Why we use information, and our legal bases#

We use personal information to answer you, to provide and fix our apps, to understand which features are used, to keep our apps and the people who use them safe, and to meet our legal obligations. If you are in the European Union, the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, or Switzerland, we rely on:

  • Contract (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR) to deliver an app or a purchase you asked for.
  • Legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f)) for usage and diagnostic reports that are on by default, so that we can find bugs and understand which features are used, and to answer messages you send us. You can object at any time; for the reports, turn them off in the app.
  • Consent (Article 6(1)(a)) where an app asks you to turn something on, or where you choose to give us information such as your contact details in a feedback form. You can withdraw consent at any time.
  • Legal obligation (Article 6(1)(c)) where the law requires us to keep records.

7. Who we share information with#

We share personal information only: with service providers that process it for us (each app's policy names them); when the law requires it; to protect people's safety or our legal rights; or as part of a sale or merger of our business, in which case this policy continues to apply to information collected under it unless you are told otherwise and given a choice where the law requires one. We do not sell personal information, we do not share it for targeted advertising, and we do not disclose it to others for their own direct marketing.

8. International transfers#

We are in the United States, so information we receive is processed there. Where European Union, United Kingdom, or Swiss law requires a safeguard for a transfer, we rely on the safeguards our service providers offer, such as the EU-US Data Privacy Framework or Standard Contractual Clauses.

9. How long we keep information#

  • Emails and feedback: for as long as we need them to answer you and to keep a record of how the request was handled, and normally no longer than 24 months after our last reply.
  • App reports and account information: for the periods stated in that app's privacy policy.
  • Information on your device or in your own iCloud storage: until you delete it. We do not hold a copy.
  • Records the law requires us to keep: for as long as that law requires.

10. Security#

We use reasonable safeguards suited to the small amount of information we hold. Information on your device is protected by Apple's app sandbox and your device's own encryption and passcode. No system is perfectly secure, and we cannot guarantee the security of information. Please keep your device's operating system up to date. If a security breach affects your personal information, we will notify you as the law requires.

11. Your rights#

11.1 Everyone

Wherever you live, you can ask us to tell you what personal information we hold about you, to correct it, or to delete it. Email contact@thomasraypublishing.com. We may need to confirm that the request comes from you, and for app reports we may need the identifier shown in that app's settings to find your records. We will not treat you differently for making a request. If we deny your request, you can appeal by replying to our answer. If we deny the appeal, we will tell you how to contact the regulator or attorney general for your area.

11.2 European Union, European Economic Area, United Kingdom, and Switzerland

You have the right to access, correct, and erase your personal data; to restrict or object to our processing of it, including processing based on legitimate interests; to receive it in a portable format; to withdraw consent where we rely on consent; and not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing that has legal or similarly significant effects (we make no such decisions). You can also complain to your data-protection authority; in the United Kingdom, that is the Information Commissioner's Office. We respond within one month. For complex requests we may extend this by up to two more months, and we will tell you why.

11.3 California and other United States states

The privacy laws of California and other states apply to businesses above certain size thresholds and may not apply to a business our size. We give residents of every state the rights in Section 11.1 anyway. We do not sell personal information or share it for cross-context behavioral advertising, and we do not use sensitive personal information to infer characteristics about you. You may use an authorized agent to make a request for you; we may ask for proof that the agent is acting for you. We respond within 45 days and may extend that once by another 45 days when reasonably necessary.

11.4 Quebec, Brazil, India, and other places with similar laws

You keep the access, correction, deletion, and portability rights that Quebec Law 25, Brazil's LGPD, India's DPDPA, and similar laws give you. Send requests to the email above. For Quebec, our person in charge of the protection of personal information is Sean Dryden.

12. Children#

Our website is for a general audience. Apart from the Trade RC password-reset page described in Section 13, which is for adults, it does not collect personal information from visitors, including children. Some of our apps may be used by children. Those apps do not require a name, email address, or other contact information to use them, and they do not show ads or track users across other companies' apps or websites. Some apps send a random app identifier with technical and usage reports, and Apple attaches an identifier for the iCloud account that created the report. We use those identifiers only to keep the app working, fix bugs, count usage, and prevent duplicate reports. We do not use them to contact anyone, build a profile, or show ads. Each app's privacy policy says whether it sends such reports and how to turn them off. Trade RC is for adults only, as its own terms explain.

If you believe a child under 13 has sent us personal information, for example through a feedback form or by email, write to contact@thomasraypublishing.com and we will delete it.

13. Our website, cookies, and browser signals#

Our website has no accounts, ads, analytics, or tracking tools, and it sets no cookies. Like every website, it is delivered by a hosting provider (currently GitHub Pages), which receives technical information such as your IP address, your browser type, and the pages you request in order to deliver the site and keep it secure. That provider handles this information under its own privacy statement. Your browser stores a small amount of information on your own device so the site works the way you chose, for example whether you paused animations. We do not receive it.

Two pages on our website help Trade RC members. The password-reset page sends your new password directly to Trade RC's sign-in provider (Supabase) and is covered by the Trade RC Privacy Policy. The email-confirmation page only shows a result and sends nothing.

Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control. Our website does not track you across other websites and does not let other companies do so, so it works the same way whether or not your browser sends a Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control signal. Our apps do not track you across other companies' apps or websites. If that ever changes, we will update this policy first and will treat a Global Privacy Control signal as a request to opt out where the law requires.

If we ever add analytics or other tools to the website, we will update this policy before we do.

14. Automated decision-making#

We do not make decisions about you by automated means that have legal or similarly significant effects. Suggestions inside our apps are advisory only.

15. Complaints#

If you believe we have mishandled your personal information, please contact us first so we can try to fix it. You can also complain to the authority for your area, for example the UK Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk), the California Privacy Protection Agency (cppa.ca.gov), the Quebec Commission d'accès à l'information (cai.gouv.qc.ca), or your national data-protection authority in the European Union.

16. Changes to this policy#

We will post any change on this page with a new version number and date. If we make a material change to how we use personal information we already hold, we will ask for your consent first where the law requires it. Each app's privacy policy explains how that app tells you about changes to its own policy.

Per-App Addendum: The Device — Cosmic Pyramid

Version 3.1.0 · Effective September 23, 2026 · Last updated September 23, 2026

The Device is a mystical entertainment app for iOS, iPadOS, and watchOS. This addendum supplements the portfolio policy above with Device-specific data flows. Where this addendum differs from the portfolio policy, this addendum controls for The Device. An equivalent version ships inside the app (Settings → About The Device → Privacy Policy).

How The Device differs from the portfolio policy#

  • What leaves your device. Most of what you do inside The Device stays on your device. Your readings (including any reflection notes), streaks, and profile settings sync between your devices through your own iCloud account, using your private iCloud database and iCloud key-value storage; TRP cannot access that data. Optional analytics, crash reports, feedback, and any referral codes you use go to our Apple-hosted CloudKit database, as described below. We don't run our own servers; that CloudKit database is the only place we hold user data.
  • CloudKit public database. The Device uses it for three narrow purposes: optional analytics and crash reports, the in-app feedback form, and referral codes you choose to create or redeem. Referral records contain a random referral identifier, the code, its zodiac prefix, and creation and redemption dates, and they are created only when you create or redeem a code. Since version 1.5 they never carry your current analytics identifier; if you created a code before 1.5, its identifier is the one your install used for analytics before that update. The analytics setting doesn't affect referral records; they exist only if you use referrals. Like every record in a CloudKit public database, all of these records are also stamped by Apple with an opaque identifier for the iCloud account that created them, which we can see.
  • Crash and performance reports. If analytics is on, The Device uploads its own crash and hang reports (technical call stacks, app and OS version, and device model — never anything you typed) to the same CloudKit database. Turning analytics off stops these uploads.
  • Name, email, and feedback. We receive your name and email only if you choose to type them into the in-app feedback form or email us. A feedback submission also carries your message, any screenshots you attach, and, unless you turn off Include Diagnostics, a diagnostics report (device, iOS and app version, storage, time zone, accessibility and app settings, subscription status, a recent-activity log, the current session identifier, and crash or performance reports stored on your device). It is sent only when you tap Send, regardless of the analytics setting.
  • Location. We do not receive your location. If you allow location access, the app sends it only to Apple's WeatherKit (see below). Because it leaves your device, our App Store privacy label lists Precise Location, not linked to you, for app functionality.
  • Analytics are pseudonymous, not anonymous. Analytics and crash records never contain your name, email, or Apple ID, but analytics reports carry the identifiers described below, which our App Store privacy label declares as “Linked to You.” We retain these records as long as they remain useful for product improvement.
  • Sources of data (CCPA). For The Device, personal information can also come from the optional analytics and crash reports and the referral codes described here, in addition to the sources in §5 above.
  • Children. The Device never requires personal information, but it does receive the pseudonymous identifiers below and, if someone chooses, a name and email through feedback.
  • Translation. The app can translate its text on your device with Apple's Translation framework after iOS downloads a language pack; no text is sent to us.
  • Policy changes. When we make a material change to this addendum, we update its version and effective date, and an equivalent version is always available in the app at Settings → About The Device → Privacy Policy.

Data stored on your device#

  • Daily prediction count (resets each day) and purchased prediction balance.
  • Total lifetime prediction count and in-app purchase transaction IDs (for deduplication).
  • Saved prediction images and reading history (stored as files, capped at 50 readings).
  • Cached daily horoscope message and recent prediction values (for diversity tracking).
  • User preferences (sound, haptics, notifications, Enhanced Awareness).
  • Optional birth date — stored on your device to personalize readings through zodiac and numerology calculations, and synced to your other devices through your own iCloud account. It is never sent to us. If you create a referral code, the code includes your zodiac sign (not your birth date).
  • Streak and milestone tracking; subscription status.
  • The random identifiers the app creates: the install identifier, the pre-1.5 one kept only so deletion can find older records, and your referral identifier and code.
  • Widget data (shared via App Group for home-screen widget display).
  • Local diagnostic logs.

Location and weather#

The Device may optionally request your location to retrieve local weather conditions via WeatherKit. The permission is optional — deny it and the app functions normally. When granted, your location is sent only to Apple's WeatherKit; weather is used to flavor prediction narratives; your location is never stored, logged, or transmitted to TRP. You can revoke location access at any time in iOS Settings.

On-device AI (Apple Foundation Models)#

The Device uses Apple's on-device Foundation Models to personalize your experience: creative prediction options, narrative intros, reading summaries, mystical reasons for lucky numbers, peril prompts, enhanced Siri responses, and reflective commentary on past readings. All AI processing occurs entirely on your device, and your prompts and outputs are never sent to us or to any third party. AI Predictions can be disabled in Settings.

Saved prediction images#

When you save a prediction reading, an image is rendered and stored in the app's local Documents directory. Sharing through the iOS share sheet is handled entirely by the operating system; TRP does not have access to shared images or their destinations.

Notifications#

Notifications are scheduled entirely on your device. The text of a notification never leaves your device: it is chosen on-device from content bundled inside the app, and it is never transmitted, logged to our database, or included in a feedback report. If analytics is on, your usage report includes notification counts only (scheduled, failed, opened, by reminder type, plus how many days of reminders are queued and how varied the copy was), your notification permission level, how many times permission was withdrawn, and the outcome of the one-time “allow banners and sounds” offer. Turning analytics off stops all of it.

Siri Shortcuts, Live Activities, widgets, and Apple Watch#

Siri Shortcuts, Live Activities, and the home-screen widgets run entirely on-device; no voice data, intent content, or on-screen content is transmitted to TRP or any third party. Streak and reading state syncs between iPhone and Apple Watch using WatchConnectivity; this sync itself sends nothing to TRP. If analytics is on, your iPhone's report includes counts of how often you open the app from Siri, widgets, Live Activities, notifications, and the Apple Watch, plus Apple Watch app use, complication taps, and Watch error counts — counts only, never content.

Privacy-minded analytics (default ON)#

While you use the app it sends individual event records (for example session start and end with the screens visited and features used, paywall views and outcomes, reflection ratings, and launch, AI, and reveal timing including battery level) in small batches and whenever the app goes to the background. Each carries the time of the event and a random session identifier that changes every time you open the app, and never your name, email, Apple ID, location, or anything you typed.

The Device also sends a usage report to Apple's CloudKit at most once every 24 hours. Reports include: app version, iOS version, device model, screen dimensions, language/locale, session count, total readings count, current and longest streaks, subscription and redemption status, purchased prediction count, feature toggle states, AI model availability, engagement counts, onboarding status, free readings remaining, purchase event count, attunement completion rate, average reading interval, most frequent prediction category, device language, sound cache performance, AI generation speed, days since install, session duration, paywall views, time-of-day usage patterns, Apple Watch companion app usage, the notification counts and permission states described above, counts of app opens from widgets, Live Activities, Siri, notifications, and the Apple Watch, App Store country, text-size setting, calendar system, whether a birth date is set (never the date), iCloud account status, translation-prompt outcomes, aggregated action counts, and error summaries.

Reports do NOT include your name, Apple ID, email address, precise location, or IP address. Each report carries two deduplication identifiers:

  • Install identifier. A random UUID, generated on a given install the first time a report is sent (only while analytics is on). It was replaced once when you updated to version 1.5, so that referral records never carry it, and you can reset it at any time in Settings → About The Device → Privacy Details. A delete-and-reinstall, or the app on a separate device, gets a different identifier; restoring a device from a backup can carry the same one over. Resetting it does not change the User-hash, so reports from before and after a reset can still be connected.
  • User-hash identifier. A salted, one-way hash derived from the opaque CloudKit user record token Apple emits for your Apple ID and this app. It is not your Apple ID and cannot be reversed to it; it lets us count unique accounts across reinstalls and devices. It is unique to this app, so it cannot be cross-referenced with other apps. Our App Store privacy label declares it as “Linked to You.” It is omitted if you are not signed in to iCloud or if analytics is off.

Apple also stamps every record in our CloudKit database (analytics reports, event and crash records, feedback, and referral records) with the same opaque account identifier the hash is derived from, and we can see it, so records uploaded from the same iCloud account can be connected to each other across sessions, reinstalls, and devices. If you send feedback with your name or email, we can connect that feedback to your other records from the same iCloud account, not only the same session. We use these identifiers solely to compute population metrics; we do not use them for advertising, to track you across apps or websites, or to build a profile of you.

Disable anytime in Settings under “Share Anonymous Analytics.” When analytics is off, nothing is sent and the User-hash is erased from your device. An install identifier created earlier stays on your device, unused, until you reset it or delete the app; resetting it or using Delete My Analytics Data may create one.

Deleting your data. You can delete this install's analytics reports and referral records in Settings → About The Device → Privacy Details → Delete My Analytics Data. For anything it can't reach (for example, reports from another device or from before an ID reset), email contact@thomasraypublishing.com with the installation ID shown on that screen and we will remove them.

Required-reason APIs#

The Device's PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy declares NSPrivacyAccessedAPICategoryUserDefaults (CA92.1, and 1C8F.1 for the App Group shared with the widget and Apple Watch), NSPrivacyAccessedAPICategoryFileTimestamp (C617.1), NSPrivacyAccessedAPICategoryDiskSpace (7D9E.1 — free storage is included only in the optional feedback diagnostics report), and NSPrivacyAccessedAPICategorySystemBootTime (35F9.1). NSPrivacyTracking is false.

Per-App Addendum: Colophon

Effective May 16, 2026

Colophon is a business-management application for independent publishers, authors, and small creative businesses (iOS, iPadOS, macOS). This addendum supplements the portfolio policy above with Colophon-specific data flows.

Data stored on your device#

  • Expense records (merchant, amount, date, category, notes, receipt images).
  • Mileage trip logs (origin, destination, distance, purpose).
  • Business documents (files, tags, categories, expiration dates).
  • Business activities and calendar-event references.
  • Journal entries (text, voice transcriptions, attendees, action items).
  • Book titles and royalty records from publishing platforms.
  • Revenue tracking data by book and platform.
  • Funding-account records (display name, institution name, optional last-four digits, payment-method type).
  • Capital contributions and tax-basis calculations.
  • App preferences and local diagnostic logs.

Funding accounts and financial data#

Colophon does not connect to any bank, access account balances, or process financial transactions. Funding-account records store only a display name, institution name, optional last-four digits, and payment-method type. Capital-contribution and tax-basis calculations run entirely on your device.

Location services#

With your permission, Colophon uses your device's location for mileage-trip detection and address-based distance calculations. Location data is processed and stored entirely on-device; it is used only for mileage logging and IRS deduction calculations.

iCloud sync#

Colophon syncs your business data across your Apple devices using Apple's NSPersistentCloudKitContainer. Your data is stored in your iCloud private database, encrypted, and not accessible to us. Sync covers expenses, trips, documents, activities, journal entries, royalty records, and funding-account data.

Calendar integration#

With your permission, Colophon reads events from Apple Calendar (via EventKit) to link business activities to calendar entries. Calendar data is read and cached on-device. Colophon may create new events when you explicitly request it; it does not modify or delete existing events without your action.

Camera, photos, OCR#

Colophon uses your camera to capture receipt images and Apple's Vision framework to perform on-device OCR for merchant, amount, and date. All image processing happens entirely on-device.

Voice recording and transcription#

The Voice-to-Journal feature uses Apple's Speech framework to transcribe voice recordings on-device. Audio is not stored permanently unless you save the transcription as a journal entry.

On-device AI (Apple Foundation Models)#

Colophon uses Apple's on-device Vision, NaturalLanguage, and Foundation Models for receipt OCR, expense categorization, journal entity extraction, and natural-language business queries. All AI processing happens entirely on-device. No prompts, inputs, or outputs are transmitted.

Anonymous analytics (default OFF — opt-in)#

Unlike Pomagotchi and The Device, Colophon's anonymous analytics are disabled by default and require your explicit opt-in. When enabled, the app sends an anonymous usage report to Apple's CloudKit at most once every 24 hours. Reports include: app version, iOS/macOS version, device model, screen dimensions, language/locale, anonymous session count, feature usage counts (number of expenses, trips, documents, journal entries), entity counts, sync health status, subscription status, and crash-free session rate.

Reports do NOT include: your name, Apple ID, email, location, business data, financial figures, merchant names, document contents, IP address, or any other personally identifiable information. Each install is assigned a random identifier for deduplication only. Enable or disable in Settings under "Share Anonymous Analytics."

Required-reason APIs#

Colophon's PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy declares NSPrivacyAccessedAPICategoryUserDefaults (CA92.1), NSPrivacyAccessedAPICategoryFileTimestamp (C617.1 and DDA9.1 for receipt-image timestamp display), NSPrivacyAccessedAPICategoryDiskSpace (E174.1), and NSPrivacyAccessedAPICategorySystemBootTime (35F9.1). NSPrivacyTracking is false.

Privacy questions?#

Email us directly. We respond within 30 days for GDPR requests and 45 days for CCPA requests.
